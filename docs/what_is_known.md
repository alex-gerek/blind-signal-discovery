# What is known: research results and repeat-work checklist

Reviewed 2026-11-28. This is an index of measured outcomes and their limits, not a new experiment, a list of universal laws or a claim that all findings are in the product. Consult it before planning work: cite the topic ID, read the source, state the new cue or changed condition, and preserve the earlier negative result.

**Evidence boundary.** The owner approved publishing the aggregate outcomes from the wider research programme on 2026-11-28. The sister-project rows below are imported reported results; their underlying private evidence was not independently re-audited in this documentation step. No closed implementation, method recipe, private dataset inventory, audio, personal location or credential is included. Historical BSD rows link public evidence at the pinned research checkpoint `9b9aa44`; later rows link their separately recorded findings and state when results are still local.

**Status vocabulary.** Measured/evidence means retained observations, not deployment. Rejected means the tested approach failed its criterion. A closed tested line needs a genuinely new cue to reopen; it is not a theorem that every method must fail. Open means a question remains unresolved. Completed runs and adopted methods are different statuses. Inspected held-out samples become development evidence for subsequent tuning.

## Before repeating a study

1. Find its BSD-/X- topic and read the cited evidence. State whether the question is already answered under the same conditions, or which new cue, data law, method or evaluation rule changes it.
2. Separate detecting an anomaly, estimating frequencies, assigning physical identity and reconstructing source waveforms. None automatically establishes the next. A good local score or aggregate AUC is not a working stem separator.
3. Preserve data-use history. Do not call an inspected control blind again. Use new independent calibration and evaluation for a changed detector; report false claims, misses, abstentions and actual exposure.
4. A source background must be independent, suitable and provenance-qualified; time separation or a non-rejected statistical test does not certify purity or exchangeability. No universal background expiry time is established here.
5. Keep rejected rules, implementation errors and unfavorable groups. Do not reopen a failed line solely to sweep another threshold until its historical case succeeds.
6. A generic idea may still be open when a specific prototype was rejected. A new experiment must state what it can distinguish that the previous one could not.

## Preservation and current open tasks

The production BSD detector remains unchanged by the latest research prototypes. L09 (calibration contamination) and L10 (shifted broadband/Union mismatch) remain open. Frequency-pair gains need independent calibration replication, broader negative controls and controlled grid/phase/amplitude follow-ups. Real-sensor error control, population sensitivity and waveform reconstruction are not established by the synthetic two-tone pilot.
